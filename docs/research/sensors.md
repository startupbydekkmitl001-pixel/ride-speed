# Phase 1 — measurement research

Research date: 30 September 2026. Scope: questions 1–14, external GNSS, measurement architecture, and expected metric accuracy. No product implementation is included.

The app can be an honest speedometer and useful ride logger with an iPhone alone. It cannot promise instrument-grade acceleration, braking or lap records across every iPhone, mount and environment. Preserve the observations, publish the method, record missing data, and keep phone and external-receiver results distinguishable. Numerical filter settings below are **proposed starting values for field validation**, not Apple guarantees, certified tolerances, legal speed limits, or universal vehicle limits.

## 1. GNSS techniques and what iPhone actually uses

| Technique | What it improves | iPhone / application access |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Multiple constellations | Availability and geometry, especially when some satellites are obscured. More satellites do not remove reflected signals automatically. | Supported systems depend on the model. Apple lists GPS, GLONASS, Galileo, QZSS and BeiDou for iPhone 14; iPhone 17 additionally lists NavIC. Apps cannot select the contributing constellation. |
| Dual frequency, commonly L1/L5 | Helps resolve ionospheric effects and improves signal robustness. It is separate from having a high output frequency in hertz. | Model dependent: Apple advertises precision dual-frequency GNSS on iPhone 14 Pro and iPhone 17, but not standard iPhone 14. An iOS 17 minimum does not imply dual-frequency hardware. |
| Doppler / carrier-derived velocity | Usually a better velocity observation than subtracting noisy position fixes. | Core Location supplies a speed estimate; Apple does **not** document that `CLLocation.speed` is always Doppler-derived or expose its internal fusion algorithm. |
| SBAS | Regional corrections/integrity information, when coverage, receiver and antenna permit. | No public Core Location control or status for SBAS. Do not claim that an iPhone uses a specific augmentation service, or that European/US coverage applies in Thailand. |
| RTK | Centimetre positioning when carrier ambiguities are fixed and corrections, antenna and sky conditions are suitable. | No public iPhone raw-GNSS/correction-injection API for implementing this with its internal receiver. Use a compatible external RTK receiver and a suitable correction source. Loss of fix must downgrade the result. |
| PPP / PPP-RTK | Precise positioning using orbit/clock and sometimes regional atmospheric corrections; convergence and service availability matter. | Not an exposed phone-only Core Location capability. An external receiver or raw-capable platform and correction products are needed. Traditional PPP can need many minutes; newer services differ. |

Apple hardware sources: [iPhone 14 specifications](https://support.apple.com/en-us/111850), [iPhone 14 Pro specifications](https://support.apple.com/en-sg/111849), [iPhone 17 specifications](https://www.apple.com/iphone-17/specs/). Technique sources: ESA Navipedia on [GNSS observables and Doppler](https://gssc.esa.int/navipedia/index.php/GNSS_Basic_Observables), [RTK](https://gssc.esa.int/navipedia/index.php/RTK_Fundamentals), [PPP](https://gssc.esa.int/navipedia/index.php/Precise_Point_Positioning), and [SBAS/EGNOS signals](https://gssc.esa.int/navipedia/index.php/EGNOS_Signal_Structure). Apple's public [speed contract](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corelocation/cllocation/speed) does not specify Doppler.

The practical priority is clear sky, a suitable mount, reliable velocity, valid timestamps and a known sensor's behaviour. RTK is valuable for racing-line position; it is not a prerequisite for useful speed or lap timing. A single-band, well-mounted dedicated receiver can outperform a dual-band phone for timing because antenna quality, update behaviour and latency also matter. This is an engineering inference, not a head-to-head benchmark of the user's devices.

## 2. Data available to an iOS app

`CLLocation` exposes latitude/longitude, altitude, horizontal and vertical uncertainty, speed and speed uncertainty, course and course uncertainty, measurement timestamp, and source information. Current APIs also include ellipsoidal altitude; check availability when targeting iOS 17. They do not expose the internal receiver's per-satellite pseudoranges, carrier phase, Doppler observables, signal strengths, satellite selection or correction engine. Android has a separate raw-GNSS API. A recent JRC research paper also explicitly identifies the absence of an equivalent iOS interface. [Apple CLLocation](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corelocation/cllocation), [Android raw GNSS](https://developer.android.com/develop/sensors-and-location/sensors/gnss), [JRC paper, 2026](https://www.mdpi.com/2673-4591/126/1/4).

Call our stored phone observations **original Core Location samples**, not raw satellite measurements. Likewise, `CMDeviceMotion` is a processed fusion output. If the test report promises raw motion, record raw accelerometer and gyro channels separately, alongside device motion and calibration metadata. Core Motion exposes both raw and processed sensor interfaces. [Apple Core Motion overview](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coremotion/).

There is no published universal “best iPhone accuracy” that can be promised. GPS.gov gives roughly 4.9 m open-sky positioning as a general smartphone example and explains that buildings, bridges and trees worsen it; this is neither an iPhone-specific bound nor a guarantee. The GPS satellite signal's range-rate accuracy must not be mistaken for the phone's speed accuracy. Centimetre RTK positions require different measurement access/hardware. [GPS.gov accuracy explanation](https://archive.gps.gov/systems/gps/performance/accuracy/), [ION study of iOS/Android internal positioning](https://www.ion.org/publications/abstract.cfm?articleID=18415).

## 3. External Bluetooth GNSS: worth supporting later

Yes. Create a receiver-neutral observation format now, but defer hardware integration until the recording and replay pipeline is proven. Prefer a receiver with documented **direct BLE** messages, measurement timestamps, velocity uncertainty, fix quality and sequence/gap information. Do not timestamp measurement truth with JavaScript receipt time. Preserve the source of every sample and make any fallback from external to internal explicit; a run should not silently mix accuracy classes.

RaceBox is the strongest first integration candidate: the maker offers its BLE protocol, and RaceChrono supports its 25 Hz stream. The protocol request page currently requires a form/email; we have not submitted it. A manufacturer-hosted revision-8 document is publicly indexed, but its freshness and compatibility must be confirmed before implementation. RaceBox Mini S adds receiver-side recording, useful when the phone disconnects; Mini does not. [RaceBox protocol access](https://www.racebox.pro/products/mini-micro-protocol-documentation), [indexed revision 8](https://www.racebox.pro/products/mini-micro-protocol-documentation?k=67c166d0bda80de96505efba), [Mini/S specifications](https://www.racebox.pro/products/racebox-mini/tech-specs).

Direct BLE is materially different from hoping iOS's system location provider preserves an accessory's advertised rate. RaceChrono reports that Garmin GLO through iOS location services is 1 Hz from iOS 18.6, and recommends RaceBox Mini/S and newer Dragy models on iOS. Core Bluetooth supports BLE accessories; arbitrary classic Bluetooth serial receivers are not automatically supported. [RaceChrono receiver recommendations](https://racechrono.com/article/faq/which-external-gps-should-i-buy), [RaceChrono support](https://racechrono.com/support), [Apple Core Bluetooth](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/documentation/NetworkingInternetWeb/Conceptual/CoreBluetooth_concepts/AboutCoreBluetooth/Introduction.html).

“u-blox based” alone is insufficient. The exact chip, firmware, antenna, enabled constellations and navigation mode determine the rate and quality. For example, MAX-M10S specifies 0.05 m/s velocity accuracy at **50% probability, 30 m/s dynamic operation**, and 1.5 m position CEP under stated conditions; its supported rates depend on configuration. These are module specifications, not a guarantee for every assembled receiver or our app. [MAX-M10S datasheet, revision 8](https://content.u-blox.com/sites/default/files/MAX-M10S_DataSheet_UBX-20035208.pdf).

## 4. Which speed observation to use

Use valid `CLLocation.speed` as the primary phone speed, retain `speedAccuracy`, and treat position-derived speed as a consistency check or a separately labelled fallback. Apple describes speed as instantaneous and informational, with a negative value meaning invalid; it warns that actual speed can change between callbacks. Its documentation does not promise a measurement algorithm. [Apple speed](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corelocation/cllocation/speed).

The reason to avoid adjacent-position differencing is error amplification. Illustratively, independent 5 m position errors at a 1 s interval give approximately `sqrt(2) × 5 / 1 ≈ 7.1 m/s` velocity uncertainty in a simple linearized model. Real errors are correlated and the Apple radius is not specified as a Gaussian standard deviation, so this is an explanation of sensitivity, not an error bound. Longer baselines reduce noise but average away acceleration and corners; short straight segments can be cross-checked against integrated speed. GNSS Doppler observes relative velocity directly, whereas summing noisy point-to-point distances also tends to inflate distance. [ESA Doppler explanation](https://gssc.esa.int/navipedia/index.php/GNSS_Basic_Observables), [Why GPS makes distances bigger than they are](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.04504).

Never fill an invalid speed with zero: zero means stationary. A stale speed becomes “—”. Position-derived estimates may remain useful for post-ride distance or coarse movement analysis when their longer interval is disclosed, but should not create a performance record when the primary velocity is missing.

## 5. Core Location setup on iOS 17+

Two supported acquisition paths should not be conflated:

| Path | Relevant controls | Proposed use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `CLLocationUpdate.liveUpdates(configuration)` | iOS 17 async sequence; predefined `.default`, `.fitness`, `.automotiveNavigation`, `.otherNavigation`, `.airborne` configurations at the iOS 17 baseline. Automatic stationary behaviour. | Good modern recording API, provided field tests confirm acceptable launch/stop behaviour. Use fitness for exercise; otherNavigation for cycling/scooters/off-road navigation; automotiveNavigation for road motor vehicles. Validate closed-course behaviour separately. |
| `CLLocationManager.startUpdatingLocation()` | `desiredAccuracy`, `distanceFilter`, `activityType`, `pausesLocationUpdatesAutomatically`, `allowsBackgroundLocationUpdates`. | Recommended initial measurement-validation path when explicit control over accuracy and automatic pausing is needed. |

`liveUpdates` does not accept `desiredAccuracy` or a sample interval. Setting those properties on a separate manager does not establish that they tune the async stream. Current documentation has newer additions such as `.maritime`; do not copy them unguarded into the iOS 17 baseline. [Apple LiveConfiguration](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corelocation/cllocationupdate/liveconfiguration), [WWDC23](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10180/), [CLLocationManager](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corelocation/cllocationmanager).

Proposed manager settings during an explicitly started recording: request precise When In Use authorization, `desiredAccuracy = kCLLocationAccuracyBest` initially; compare BestForNavigation during measurement tests; `distanceFilter = kCLDistanceFilterNone`; suitable `activityType`; automatic pausing disabled for performance tests. None guarantees accuracy or a fixed 1 Hz rate. Raising desired accuracy cannot override reduced-accuracy authorization. Stop location services at session end. [Desired accuracy](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corelocation/cllocationmanager/desiredaccuracy), [automatic pausing](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corelocation/cllocationmanager/pauseslocationupdatesautomatically).

For manager delivery, enable `UIBackgroundModes: location` and `allowsBackgroundLocationUpdates` when recording starts in the foreground. For the async path, create and strongly retain `CLBackgroundActivitySession`, also with the location background mode; invalidate it at the end. A user-initiated foreground session can continue with When In Use authorization and the visible location indicator; Always is not a magic guarantee of permanent execution. [Manager background delivery](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corelocation/cllocationmanager/allowsbackgroundlocationupdates), [background activity session](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corelocation/clbackgroundactivitysession-3mzv3).

The iOS 17 `isStationary` flag and newer `stationary`/diagnostic APIs need version-aware handling. A stationary pause, system termination, permission change and deliberate force-quit are different states. Save the session identity and restore supported sessions promptly on relaunch, without claiming an uninterrupted stream. Apple's WWDC24 session explicitly says Core Location need not keep the process continuously running when it has no data to deliver. [CLLocationUpdate](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corelocation/cllocationupdate), [WWDC24 authorization and lifecycle](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2024/10212/).

## 6. Rejecting bad readings without inventing good ones

Apple defines negative `speedAccuracy` as an invalid speed; nonnegative values describe plus/minus uncertainty in m/s. A negative `horizontalAccuracy` invalidates the coordinate. These values are estimates, not integrity certificates; Apple does not specify a confidence percentage on these pages. [Speed accuracy](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corelocation/cllocation/speedaccuracy), [horizontal accuracy](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corelocation/cllocation/horizontalaccuracy).

Proposed pipeline, retaining every original sample and every rejection reason:

1. Validate finite coordinates, speed, uncertainty and timestamps. Distinguish coordinate validity from speed validity. Sort batched observations by measurement time, deduplicate repeated timestamps, and flag out-of-order/restarted clocks. A cached startup fix must not appear as live data; Apple documents that cached values may arrive first. [Delegate behaviour](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corelocation/cllocationmanagerdelegate).
2. For the live display, start with age no greater than 2 s, nonnegative speed and speed accuracy, and full-accuracy permission. Flag implausibly future-dated samples; preserve wall-clock and monotonic receipt clocks to detect clock changes. Do not reject historically valid samples merely because background delivery was delayed: their *live freshness* and *historical measurement validity* are separate.
3. Proposed quality bands: good speed uncertainty ≤0.5 m/s with coordinate uncertainty ≤10 m; weak ≤1.5 m/s and ≤25 m; otherwise unavailable. The main speed shows “—” whenever the data fails the good-data gate; quality details can explain weak reception without promoting an unreliable number to the main display. These are gates to test, not a claim that good equals ±0.5 m/s in reality. Cycling at low speed needs stricter scrutiny because 0.5 m/s is already 1.8 km/h.
4. Compare consecutive valid velocities using an uncertainty allowance: only treat `|v2-v1| > aLimit × Δt + u1 + u2` as a plausibility flag. Check several samples and IMU context. Do not clamp the observation to a vehicle maximum or smooth a spike into a plausible record.
5. Use a small robust outlier detector and a light display filter. Keep display filtering separate from measurements used for event timing. Record the filter version and delay. A Kalman filter is an option after error statistics are measured; it does not create missing information.
6. In tunnels or severe multipath, mark gaps, suppress records, and restart tracking conservatively after several mutually consistent fixes. Do not draw a straight segment through a long gap and count it as observed distance. Short IMU extrapolation may animate a labelled estimate but must not establish an official max or performance result.

Source simulation is another independent quality dimension. `isSimulatedBySoftware = true` detects Apple's on-device software simulation, including debugger GPX simulation. It does not establish that `false` means authentic, and absence of source metadata is not proof of authenticity. Preserve accessory provenance separately. [Apple simulation flag](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corelocation/cllocationsourceinformation/issimulatedbysoftware).

## 7. Trustworthy maximum speed

Define two different quantities: a private diagnostic **observed peak** and the primary **verified sustained max**. Proposed sustained max is the highest **minimum accepted speed** in any contiguous 3 s window, with valid observations bracketing the full window, at least four observations for a roughly 1 Hz stream, normal sample spacing, no source transition/gap, and no unresolved acceleration inconsistency. Require valid observations before and after the window before confirming it. A high point alone cannot become the window's minimum. Start with `speedAccuracy ≤0.5 m/s`, retain window uncertainty and coverage, and tune against a reference receiver. This conservative lower envelope is a product definition, not a statistical confidence bound or proof of what happened between samples.

Run quality/consistency checks before window calculation. A mean or median alone would not prove spike resistance: a plausible accepted high point can lift a mean. Test not only an isolated spike but a two-sample plateau, believable slow drift, prolonged spoofing, low-speed jitter and reacquisition after a tunnel. Label the chosen statistic “Speed held for 3 s” in the UI and explain that it takes the lowest accepted observation in each 3 s window, then the highest of those windows. It intentionally underestimates short peaks and is not the fastest instantaneous speed the vehicle reached. Long coherent falsification still requires separate integrity checks.

For rankings, use the same duration and validation version for every source class, retain original data around the window, and consider overlapping uncertainty ranges a tie instead of rewarding meaningless decimals. Do not subtract an uncertainty number and label the result a certified lower bound without validating its coverage. This policy is our proposed design. The failure it addresses is documented by Strava: coordinate errors can inflate max speed even when a smoothed graph looks normal. [Strava speed calculations](https://support.strava.com/en-us/articles/15401804-moving-time-speed-and-pace-calculations).

## 8. Realistic vehicle envelopes

There is no valid universal “bicycle < X, car < Y” physical rule. Surface grip, grade, tyres, power, rider position, downforce and specialty vehicles change the envelope. E-bike assistance cut-off is not a maximum possible downhill speed. Bosch, for example, specifies assistance up to 25 km/h for one motor; it is not a global classification rule. [Bosch Performance Line CX](https://www.bosch-ebike.com/en/products/performance-line-cx).

Use the following as **deliberately broad beta review thresholds**, not as rejection/clamping limits or asserted population statistics. Acceleration is sustained longitudinal acceleration, after orientation/gravity processing, not an instantaneous bump peak.

| Selected vehicle | Review sustained speed above | Review sustained forward acceleration above | Review sustained braking magnitude above | Interpretation |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Bicycle | 100 km/h | 0.5 g | 0.9 g | Downhill/sprint/special bicycles require context; a large vertical shock is a different event. |
| E-bike | 100 km/h | 0.7 g | 1.0 g | Ask later for pedal-assist vs high-power motorcycle-like machines; do not infer class from assistance limits. |
| Motorcycle | 350 km/h | 1.5 g | 1.6 g | Performance and racing motorcycles need a different review policy from commuter scooters. |
| Car | 400 km/h | 2.0 g | 2.0 g | Hypercars and race cars can legitimately trigger review; racing categories need separate calibration. |

These are proposed false-spike screening settings, not legal advice or established limits. A published bicycle study discusses roughly 0.5 g emergency braking and a geometry-dependent theoretical example of 0.56 g; it does not establish a universal bicycle limit. Rimac reports 100–0 km/h in 28.96 m, corresponding to about 1.36 g average deceleration under a constant-deceleration calculation. That alone disproves treating 1 g as an absolute car-braking maximum. [Bicycle braking study](https://bikeleague.org/sites/default/files/Joganich%20%282018%29%20A%20video-based%20system%20for%20measuring%20the%20braking%20performance%20of%20a%20bicycle_SAE%202018-01-5032.pdf), [Rimac test results](https://www.rimac-automobili.com/bendingphysics/episode-1.php).

Extreme examples illustrate why review is preferable to clipping: Guinness documents a 296.009 km/h slipstream bicycle ride, with towing and specialised equipment; Bugatti documented 490.484 km/h in a special test. Neither belongs in an ordinary bicycle/road leaderboard. Vehicle identity still cannot be proven by sensor thresholds. [Guinness bicycle record context](https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/news/2018/9/a-history-of-cycling-speed-records-as-denise-mueller-koronek-reaches-183-mph-541481), [Bugatti test](https://newsroom.bugatti.com/en/press-releases/bugatti-world-record-lower-saxony-versus-nevada).

## 9. Acceleration and braking timing

Approximately 1 Hz is an observed common phone-delivery pattern, not Apple's guaranteed rate. A naive first-sample-above-threshold timer has up to one sample interval of delay at each endpoint; its duration error is the difference of those endpoint errors. Missing samples and internal latency can make the error larger. Do not claim every 1 Hz result is “accurate to 1 s” or that interpolation makes it accurate to 0.01 s.

For a monotonic threshold crossing bracketed by valid `(t1,v1)` and `(t2,v2)`, linear interpolation uses `tCross = t1 + (vTarget-v1)/(v2-v1) × (t2-t1)`. This removes sample-grid rounding if velocity varies linearly between observations. It does not remove velocity bias, timestamp lag, wheel slip in a reference sensor, or nonlinear acceleration. A first-order uncertainty sensitivity is `δt ≈ δv / |dv/dt|`; 0.5 m/s speed uncertainty at 3 m/s² is already about 0.17 s at one crossing, before other errors. This is a model calculation, not an Apple tolerance. Apple's speed documentation explicitly warns about changes between updates. [Apple speed](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corelocation/cllocation/speed).

True zero is especially difficult: a phone can remain clamped at zero briefly after moving, jitter while still, and cannot observe the exact start between sparse fixes. For 0–100, arm after a stationary interval, use mount-calibrated motion to estimate launch onset, anchor short-term integrated motion to GNSS, and reject uncertain launches. For 100–0, interpolate the 100 crossing and combine a sustained stationary confirmation with motion to estimate stop time. Count confirmation delay separately from estimated stop time. Until benchmarked, call these **estimated phone tests**, with uncertainty and no competitive hundredth-second claims.

IMU fusion improves temporal detail but introduces bias, mount, gravity, clock-alignment and drift errors. A 1° attitude error leaks about 0.017 g, or 0.17 m/s², from gravity into an axis; over 5 s that is roughly 0.86 m/s velocity error if not corrected. The proper solution is a short-window estimator with GNSS updates and calibrated delay, not free-running double integration. High-rate external GNSS reduces interpolation intervals and provides a much stronger basis, but its velocity and latency still need validation.

One-foot rollout excludes the first 0.3048 m from the timed interval. It changes the definition of the result; it is not an accuracy enhancement. Keep rollout and true-start leaderboards separate and store the setting. RACELOGIC documents its rollout behaviour. Rimac's quoted 1.81 s 0–100 figure explicitly uses a high-friction surface and one-foot rollout, so comparisons must preserve conditions. [RACELOGIC rollout](https://en.racelogic.support/automotive/data-loggers/pbt/ug/user-interface/accel-settings/), [Rimac specifications](https://web-cdn.rimac-automobili.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/20171226/Nevera_Technical-specifications.pdf).

Dragy and RaceBox combine a dedicated receiver, antenna and performance software, with newer products offering up to 25 Hz and IMUs. Their exact estimation, lag compensation and quality algorithms are not fully published; do not assert a proprietary fusion method. RaceBox advertises timing agreement and very small location errors, but supplies no universal confidence bound on the cited product page. RACELOGIC's 10 Hz Micro datasheet usefully distinguishes 0.01 s displayed resolution, 0.1 s acceleration/braking accuracy, and 0.01 s lap timing specification. That distinction should be copied into our product language. [Dragy Pro](https://www.godragy.com/dragy-pro/), [RaceBox Mini](https://www.racebox.pro/products/racebox-mini), [RACELOGIC Micro datasheet](https://www.racelogic.co.uk/_downloads/vbox/Datasheets/Data_Loggers/RLVBMIC01_Data.pdf).

## 10. Longitudinal and lateral G with an arbitrary mount

An arbitrary **fixed** mount is workable. A phone loose in a pocket or being handled is not a trustworthy vehicle accelerometer. Core Motion's `userAcceleration` removes its estimate of gravity; `attitude`, `gravity` and `rotationRate` are available. Device axes are not vehicle axes. [CMDeviceMotion](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coremotion/cmdevicemotion).

Proposed calibration and processing:

1. Record 3–5 s stationary to estimate residual bias, noise and a gravity-aligned vertical axis. Record the initial mount transform. This does not reveal the vehicle's forward yaw by itself.
2. Resolve forward direction using a user-aligned mount axis and a subsequent straight, steady run with trustworthy GNSS course. Stationary GNSS course is not usable. GPS course gives direction of motion, not the phone's pointing direction; therefore course alone cannot determine an unknown phone-to-vehicle rotation without a reference frame or an observed straight acceleration. A true-north attitude reference may help, but magnetometers near vehicles can be distorted. [Course versus heading](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corelocation/getting-heading-and-course-information), [attitude reference frames](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coremotion/cmattitudereferenceframe).
3. Rotate acceleration into a gravity-aligned navigation frame, then project onto horizontal forward and lateral directions. Use a low-drift attitude reference plus GNSS yaw correction when valid; never silently substitute compass heading for velocity course. Define signs: positive longitudinal = accelerating, negative = braking, lateral sign = left/right consistently.
4. On motorcycles and bicycles, account for lean: a device-frame lateral axis is not horizontal cornering acceleration. Bank, suspension motion and steering-mounted phone rotation need explicit handling. Compare sustained lateral acceleration against `speed × course angular-rate` as a rough independent check; 1 Hz course cannot validate fast transients. Keep a G-G plot's interpretation separate from rider-felt load or tyre friction utilisation.
5. Start with 25 Hz device motion while recording and assess 50 Hz for performance tests, following the canonical test plan. Consider 100 Hz only if comparison demonstrates useful additional information within the battery budget. These are requested rates; use delivered timestamps. Start with a 2–3 Hz low-pass for car manoeuvre display and 1–2 Hz for rough bicycle/motorcycle data; keep the higher-rate original and avoid filtering away short brake events. Filtering adds lag and attenuates peaks, so timing must account for it. [Requested versus actual motion intervals](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coremotion/cmmotionmanager/devicemotionupdateinterval).
6. Detect abrupt mount shifts, clipping, vibration bursts, magnetic problems and missing samples; invalidate the calibrated G result until confidence recovers. At rest the linear G display should be near zero, while a raw accelerometer magnitude near 1 g is expected. Show “—” when alignment is unknown.

The rate/filter choices are engineering hypotheses. A bicycle-mounted smartphone study uses vertical acceleration to detect road roughness, confirming that bumps create real, large motion features needing separate treatment. [Bicycle road-surface study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5876687/). A shared timebase must align Core Motion's uptime timestamps with location observation time; retain clock anchors and uncertainty, not just callback arrival times.

## 11. Hard-brake detection

Use event detection, not a single `acceleration < threshold` sample. The initial thresholds below are **tunable product definitions**; no universal standard maps a vehicle class to one correct value. “Hard braking” does not mean collision, danger or fault.

| Vehicle | Candidate longitudinal deceleration | Minimum duration | Initial extra evidence |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Bicycle | ≤−0.30 g | 0.4 s | Valid mount, initial speed ≥3 m/s, consistent subsequent speed loss. |
| E-bike | ≤−0.30 g | 0.4 s | Same, with class-specific tuning after actual bikes are known. |
| Motorcycle | ≤−0.40 g | 0.4 s | Lean-aware transform, no mount motion, coherent braking profile. |
| Car | ≤−0.40 g | 0.4 s | Grade/gravity correction, coherent speed reduction. |

Proposed end hysteresis: end when longitudinal deceleration rises above −0.15 g for 0.5 s; merge adjacent fragments within 1 s. Require net speed reduction consistent with the integrated longitudinal signal, allowing GNSS uncertainty and lag. A vertical impulse followed by rebound, with little sustained forward speed change, is likely a bump; mark it separately. A real brake on a bump can contain both, so a blanket vertical-acceleration veto is wrong. At 1 Hz, confirm a short event after the fact rather than claiming its exact boundaries from GNSS alone.

A 2026 bicycle study found a root threshold around −2.94 m/s² in its dataset, supporting −0.30 g as an initial bicycle candidate, not a universal calibrated cutoff. NHTSA discusses different braking levels in different test contexts. Google's smartphone hard-braking research uses a learned model and vehicle-sensor labels, illustrating why thresholds alone do not prove events. [Cycling manoeuvre paper](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/26/16/5138), [NHTSA discussion](https://www.nhtsa.gov/sites/nhtsa.gov/files/2024-11/NCAP-Final-Decision-Notice-Advanced-Driver-Assistance-Systems-Roadmap-11182024-web.pdf), [Smartphone hard-braking research](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.01934).

## 12. Altitude and elevation gain/loss

Use the barometer primarily for relative change, and a trustworthy altitude reference for the baseline and slow drift correction. `CMAltimeter` has both relative and absolute updates; always query each availability function and handle permission denial. `NSMotionUsageDescription` is required. `CMAbsoluteAltitudeData` is documented for iPhone 12 and later, with sea-level altitude, an accuracy estimate in metres at one standard deviation, and a separate recommended precision. Its precision is not its accuracy. [CMAltimeter](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coremotion/cmaltimeter), [CMAbsoluteAltitudeData](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coremotion/cmabsolutealtitudedata).

Proposed elevation estimator:

- Preserve pressure, relative altitude, absolute-altitude values/uncertainty, and GNSS altitude/vertical uncertainty independently.
- Anchor relative altitude to a known surveyed height when available; otherwise use a stable, good absolute/GNSS baseline. Correct long-term pressure drift slowly rather than repeatedly jumping to each noisy GNSS altitude.
- Smooth in distance/time and use turning-point hysteresis. Start with a 3 m reversal threshold for good barometric data and a larger 10 m threshold for GNSS/DEM-only estimates, adjusted for observed noise. Accumulate meaningful rises and falls once; never sum every positive/negative sample difference. Publish the method because shallow rolling terrain can be suppressed.
- Compute min/max from the validated smoothed profile, not the raw extremes. Preserve corrections and allow reprocessing.
- Mark suspect pressure changes caused by weather, airflow, moving between indoor/outdoor spaces, or vehicle cabin pressure. Relative altitude can drift without physical ascent.

A digital elevation model can improve absolute ground-height estimates after the ride, but road bridges, elevated expressways, tunnels, cliffs, vegetation and inaccurate coordinates can assign the wrong surface. DEM data must have known resolution, vertical datum, licensing and Thai coverage; do not silently replace barometric bridge height with terrain beneath it. Keep “measured” and “corrected” profiles distinct. Apple distinguishes sea-level `altitude` from WGS84 ellipsoidal height, so datum conversion must be explicit before combining external data. [CLLocation altitude definitions](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corelocation/cllocation).

Strava currently reports a >2 m consistency threshold with strong barometric data and >10 m without, and generally corrects mobile-app elevation using its elevation database. Garmin normally preserves recorded barometric elevations and enables survey-based corrections by default without a barometer; compatible devices calibrate against known elevation, DEM or GNSS. Their specific databases are not automatically available free to our app. [Strava elevation](https://support.strava.com/en-us/articles/15402093-elevation-on-strava-faqs), [Garmin elevation corrections](https://support.garmin.com/en-US/?faq=R4I5hFFcUk8gJPC4zi0Xv6), [Garmin barometer calibration and disturbances](https://support.garmin.com/en-GB/?faq=sFMkjQFdnZ99DcunfAue66&tab=topics).

## 13. Moving time, stopped time and auto-pause

Keep recording during an automatic pause. Auto-pause should classify time and the visible timer, not destroy observations needed to correct a false stop. Use four states: moving, stopped, manually paused, and unknown. Elapsed time continues from session start to finish; moving and stopped time must not silently absorb unknown gaps. Define average moving speed as accepted moving distance / moving time, and separately expose elapsed average when useful.

Proposed initial hysteresis for all vehicles: enter moving above 1.0 m/s for 2 s; enter stopped below 0.5 m/s for 3 s with corroborating low motion. Use interval uncertainty: if speed±uncertainty straddles the decision boundary, defer certainty. Permit slow cycling/crawling mode after testing, since a car threshold can erase a bicycle climbing slowly. Retrospectively assign the transition to the best supported boundary, not the end of its confirmation timer. Do not pause an acceleration test or lap clock because the vehicle stops.

These settings are an app policy, not Strava's documented numerical thresholds. Strava describes GPS-based cycling auto-pause and explains that platforms can differ in moving time; it uses elapsed time for competitive segments/best efforts. That supports explicitly separating ride convenience metrics from competitive clocks. [Strava auto-pause](https://support.strava.com/en-us/articles/15402141-auto-pause), [Strava time definitions](https://support.strava.com/en-us/articles/15401804-moving-time-speed-and-pace-calculations).

## 14. Laps and comparison

Proposed start/finish algorithm: store a finite line across the intended course in local metre coordinates. For successive accepted positions on opposite sides, compute the segment/line intersection fraction and interpolate the two **measurement** timestamps. Require correct crossing direction, intersection within gate width, a minimum lap duration/distance, and sufficient along-course speed. A point merely near the line is insufficient. Reject crossings spanning a gap, poor fixes, a source change, or an ambiguous turnaround. Use a geofence only to wake/arm logic, not for precise finish timing.

An approximate timing sensitivity is position uncertainty normal to the gate divided by velocity normal to it. With a 5 m error and 10 m/s normal speed the sensitivity is about 0.5 s for a crossing. Near-parallel crossings are ill-conditioned. A fixed location bias may cancel between repeated lap crossings, but that is not guaranteed when satellite errors, racing lines or conditions change. Interpolation and relative cancellation explain why timing can be finer than the update interval; RACELOGIC explicitly interpolates lap/split times and specifies conditions for its timing figures. [VBOX lap-timer manual](https://www.racelogic.co.uk/_downloads/vbox/Manuals/Data_Loggers/RLVBLAP01-English.pdf), [VBOX Video HD2 specifications](https://www.racelogic.co.uk/_downloads/vbox/Datasheets/Data_Loggers/VBOX_VIDEO_DATA.pdf).

Distance auto-laps interpolate the crossing of each cumulative accepted-distance boundary; a measurement gap makes the boundary uncertain. Manual laps use the input event timestamp and remain marked manual; human reaction and UI/event delivery affect them, so do not mix them into automatic-lap rankings.

For best-lap delta, align current position to a monotonically advancing reference-course coordinate with a search window, heading check and distance tolerance. Interpolate reference time at equivalent course progress, then subtract it from current elapsed lap time. Do not use nearest point globally on hairpins, crossings or adjacent track sections; do not simply compare percentages of differently driven lap distance. Hide delta when alignment is ambiguous. VBOX describes position-based delta to avoid problems from taking different lines. RaceChrono warns that bad fixes near the finish can merge laps. [VBOX predictive timing](https://www.vboxmotorsport.co.uk/us/predictive-lap-timing), [RaceChrono support](https://racechrono.com/support).

## Architecture implications

The recommended change to the proposed stack is **native durable acquisition, then TypeScript analysis**. Batching native events into JS is good for foreground UI performance, but JS cannot be the only owner of samples that must survive suspension, bridge delays, reloads or process termination. Apple describes background delivery as event/lifecycle dependent, and Bluetooth background support also permits termination. [WWDC24 lifecycle](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2024/10212/), [Core Bluetooth background execution](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/documentation/NetworkingInternetWeb/Conceptual/CoreBluetooth_concepts/CoreBluetoothBackgroundProcessingForIOSApps/PerformingTasksWhileYourAppIsInTheBackground.html).

Proposed separation:

- Swift owns sensor lifetimes, permissions, native receipt clocks, session markers, sequence IDs, bounded buffers and durable append-only chunks or a native SQLite writer. Persist original observations before acknowledging delivery to JS; avoid a JS/native concurrent-writer design without explicit ownership. Commit in bounded batches, document the maximum crash-loss window, and recover incomplete sessions.
- Give the active native journal and any database sidecars an explicit file-protection policy that permits recording after screen lock. `FileProtectionType.completeUntilFirstUserAuthentication` remains accessible after the first unlock following boot; `complete` does not. Check the actual files and test creation, append, flush and recovery while locked; preserve encryption and explain the chosen access policy. [Apple file protection](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundation/fileprotectiontype/completeuntilfirstuserauthentication).
- TypeScript owns versioned pure filters, quality decisions, statistics, timing and deterministic replay. Run it live while the runtime is available, and replay native chunks after resume for authoritative summaries. Preserve all inputs and calibration metadata. Windows unit tests can cover the same pure math without pretending to validate the native sensors.
- If a future feature needs guaranteed immediate background feedback, its minimum state machine must move native or be explicitly foreground-only. Keeping *all* real-time processing in JS and promising an always-running background G/timing engine are incompatible requirements.
- The UI consumes reduced-rate summaries; interpolation may animate a needle but must not manufacture additional measurement samples. Raw recording cadence, processing cadence and rendering cadence are separate.
- Stop every sensor at session end. Persist per-channel availability/denial/gap status; Core Location support does not guarantee uninterrupted motion or barometer callbacks during every background state. iOS can terminate even correctly configured code, and the user's force-quit must be treated as an interruption.
- Replay and imported GPX are analysis inputs with explicit provenance. GPX often lacks velocity uncertainty, IMU, source flags and timing precision; do not fabricate those fields or let replay qualify for a live record.

## Required measurement validation before later phases

1. Begin with the confirmed iPhone 14 Plus on iOS 26 and PCX160, S1000RR and Civic RS profiles in the [device/vehicle matrix](../TEST_PLAN.md#confirmed-device-and-vehicles). Record the exact iOS patch/build, cases, mounts and other testers' phones. Include an older compatible phone if the group uses one. Capture native API availability and observed sensor rates; do not assume dual-frequency GNSS for the 14 Plus.
2. Test stationary outdoors, walking/crawling, constant speeds, stop/start, trees, tall buildings and tunnels. Compare against a co-mounted reference GNSS logger; a car dashboard speedometer is a useful sanity check, not ground truth.
3. Use controlled facilities for acceleration/braking/lap testing, with repeat runs in both directions and known rollout settings. Align traces by timestamp and estimate delay separately from value bias. Report sample size, median/95th-percentile error, missed-event rate, outliers, and how often reported uncertainty covers reference error.
4. Test mount rotations, a deliberate mount shift while stopped, smooth versus rough surfaces and motorcycle lean, subject to the vehicle-specific mounting guidance in the test plan. Do not directly mount the iPhone on the S1000RR for these tests; vehicle-axis G requires suitable independently mounted motion equipment. Label brake and bump events separately. Do not ask friends to perform emergency braking on public roads merely to tune thresholds.
5. Compare elevation against a known-height hill or surveyed endpoints; do repeat loops and a stationary weather-drift test. Report total gain/loss and return-to-start height error separately.
6. Record foreground, locked screen, background, low-power mode, incoming calls, denied/revoked permissions, Metro reload, OS termination, force-quit, full storage and reconnecting an accessory. Verify native sample continuity and truthful gap recovery; passing a foreground replay does not validate background capture.
7. Measure battery and thermal effects by device under fixed brightness and conditions. Use the canonical [test plan](../TEST_PLAN.md) for all performance targets: initially no more than 8 battery percentage points/hour locked and 15/hour with the speed screen at moderate brightness, explicitly unmeasured. Full sunlight brightness, weak GNSS and high-rate motion can exceed those targets. Record actual results before publishing an efficiency claim.

Known unknowns: Apple's speed/fusion/latency implementation; actual phone-only error distributions for the group's devices in Thailand; background motion completeness after free-ID sideloading on their exact iOS versions; receiver-specific protocol access and timebase semantics; barometric drift in the mounts/cabins used; and validated event/classification thresholds for the confirmed PCX160, S1000RR and Civic RS profiles. Model names alone do not resolve these thresholds. These require device tests in the next authorised phase, not more confident prose.
