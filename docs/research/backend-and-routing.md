# Phase 1: routing, verification, backend, storage, and privacy

Research date: **2026-09-30**. Scope: brief questions **15 and 20–23**; private testing with 1–10 friends, iOS 17+, Thailand first, worldwide use later, and a hard requirement to remain on free plans. This is documentary research and a proposed architecture. **No route, signed iPhone build, verification workload, compression ratio, or backend deployment was empirically tested.** Provider limits can change; recheck before their implementation phase.

Recommended direction: keep full rides locally; use **Supabase Free** for invited accounts, compact leaderboard records, private evidence files, and bounded server verification. Use native Apple maps and car directions; evaluate native cycling with the actual build SDK and an iOS 17 device before relying on it. ORS is a promising bicycle/e-bike fallback, subject to its current data-use terms. **Geoapify has an explicit motorcycle profile and a free plan**, making it a Phase 5 candidate; Thai route correctness, language support and hard-zero-cost terms still need resolution. Public performance rankings should require an approved closed course.

## 15. Free route planning

### The important Apple cycling compatibility distinction

Apple introduced cycling requests to MapKit in its **WWDC25** presentation. Current `MKDirectionsTransportType.cycling` documentation exists; its official DocC metadata reports **iOS 14.0+**, even though older SDKs did not expose the symbol. Thus neither “MapKit cannot do cycling” nor “requires iOS 26 at runtime” is an adequate conclusion. Use a modern Xcode SDK that exposes it, then test successful requests on an actual iOS 17 device. The backwards deployment annotation alone is not an observed success result. Apple’s current consumer feature list includes **Thailand** for cycling; that list concerns current Apple Maps availability, not an iOS 17 app compatibility test. Sources: [WWDC25 MapKit, cycling at 16:06](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/204/), [cycling API](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/mapkit/mkdirectionstransporttype/cycling), [official availability metadata](https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/documentation/mapkit/mkdirectionstransporttype/cycling.json), [country availability](https://www.apple.com/ios/feature-availability/#maps-cycling).

### ORS terms need a deliberate implementation decision

The current HeiGIT terms, read in the actual browser because the page is client-rendered, require ORS/HeiGIT and OpenStreetMap attribution and state that ORS results use **CC-BY-SA 4.0**. They also prohibit transmitting personal data except account-management data, limit each person to one account, and permit blocking repeated quota abuse. This differs from older material describing CC-BY alone. **Do not upload identifiable ride traces, user IDs, or home addresses to ORS.** Even a location-only request can be personal data in context; using a proxy does not by itself settle the terms. Before Phase 5, establish a permitted route-planning use involving user-selected public waypoints, or keep ORS disabled. This is a contractual applicability question left open here, not a claim that consent overrides the provider’s terms. [Current HeiGIT terms](https://account.heigit.org/info/tos).

For GraphHopper, the personal, noncommercial friend test fits the stated Free purpose in principle; a commercial release would need a different entitlement. Its terms also need review before retaining or redistributing route responses. [GraphHopper terms](https://www.graphhopper.com/terms/).

Geoapify is promising but has two explicit gates. Its instruction-language list currently **does not include Thai**: translate structured maneuvers in the app with native-speaker review, or clearly expose an English-instruction fallback. Its current pricing page permits Free commercial production, while the linked 2024 terms reserve suspension **or overage charges** after quota excess and mention production limitations. For a hard-zero-cost promise, clarify how those terms apply to a no-card Free account before enabling it, and enforce an application quota safely below the provider limit. Do not quietly enroll in a paid subscription or assume missing card details remove contractual obligations. [Routing language enum](https://apidocs.geoapify.com/docs/routing/), [Free plan FAQ](https://www.geoapify.com/pricing/), [Geoapify terms](https://www.geoapify.com/terms-and-conditions/).

### Recommendation and Thai route test gate

**Proposed design:** separate map display, routing, and route following behind distinct interfaces. Start with Apple map display and native automobile routing. Attempt native cycling only after the compatibility test above; retain a provider fallback rather than silently replacing cycling with walking. Evaluate ORS regular/road/electric cycling and Geoapify bicycle/motorcycle routing after their gates above. Until motorcycle validation passes, support free riding and imported/user-reviewed GPX routes; do not label an automobile route “motorcycle safe.” Car roads and motorcycle access restrictions can differ, and simply avoiding tolls is not sufficient evidence of legal access. The existing ORS team discussion explains why bicycle routing is not a motorcycle substitute. [ORS motorcycle discussion](https://ask.openrouteservice.org/t/adding-motorcycle-profile/3928).

**Accuracy in Thailand is unknown until tested.** Coverage is not a measurement of legal access, route desirability, turn accuracy, or ETA quality. Neither Apple’s feature page nor OSM-based provider documentation establishes a Thailand head-to-head winner. ORS explicitly disclaims guaranteed route/data quality. [Apple coverage](https://www.apple.com/ios/feature-availability/), [HeiGIT routing disclaimer](https://account.heigit.org/info/tos).

Before shipping routes, inspect at least 20 known routes covering Bangkok elevated roads/frontage roads, ordinary streets, cycling paths, pedestrian-only bridges, unpaved roads, ferry crossings, rural hills, and one country outside Thailand. Record provider, profile, SDK/iOS version, route date, illegal/inappropriate segments, missing turns, detour ratio, and rerouting latency. For motorcycles, use official/local access information and rider review; do not test questionable instructions by following them. Run an airplane-mode case to distinguish a cached line from genuine offline recalculation. All of this is a proposed test plan, not completed validation.

Use one backend-held provider key, per-member request caps, input limits, and a global quota counter; cache only as terms permit. Never make route calls at GPS sample rate. Ten friends at 10 route calculations/day require **100/day**, before retries and reroutes; this is arithmetic, not observed use. Reserve headroom and stop automatic retries on quota errors.

## 20. Cheating resistance without App Attest

### Honest trust boundary

Server recomputation prevents a client from merely submitting an invented final speed or time. **It cannot prove that client-supplied raw samples occurred physically.** A modified app can synthesize coherent GPS, motion, timestamps, source flags, and vehicle declarations. Hashes and locally held signing keys protect consistency or account possession; they do not replace Apple attestation. Apple’s App Attest protocol validates an app instance/key using an attestation and one-time server challenges, a materially stronger origin signal that is absent from this proposal. [Apple server-validation model](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/devicecheck/validating-apps-that-connect-to-your-server).

Therefore label results **“server checked”**, with a separate measurement-quality rating and review state; do not call them “cheat proof,” “certified,” or “verified GPS authenticity.” Start with invited friends and human review of new records. General public competition is a later trust problem, not solved by this phase.

### Source and activity signals

| Signal | What is documented | Proposed handling and limitation |
|---|---|---|
| `sourceInformation.isSimulatedBySoftware` | True when Core Location generates a location through on-device software simulation, e.g. Xcode GPX simulation; default false. | Any true sample makes that evidence ineligible for competitive records. Preserve it in replay/debug logs. False or unavailable does **not** prove no spoofing; a client can lie and the flag is not documented as a detector of every attack. |
| `sourceInformation.isProducedByAccessory` | True can mean an external accessory, including an MFi GPS accessory or CarPlay. | Store separately. Do not reject as cheating or infer “25 Hz GNSS” from it. It does not identify receiver quality, actual output frequency, or protocol. |
| `CMMotionActivity` | Has cycling, automotive, walking, running, stationary, unknown, with confidence. Flags need not be mutually exclusive. | Use confidence-weighted time fractions as review signals. There is no dedicated e-bike or motorcycle classifier, and stationary+automotive can be valid at traffic lights. |
| Speed, acceleration, G, altitude, course | Phone-derived measurements have errors and depend on sampling, mounting, filtering, and terrain. | Check cross-sensor consistency, prolonged engine-like acceleration, implausible discontinuities, repeated identical traces, and mismatch with declared class. These are proposed heuristics; do not auto-convict a fast downhill cyclist or distinguish every e-bike from every bicycle. |

### Proposed verification pipeline

The following is an architecture proposal; thresholds and runtime are unmeasured.

1. **Enroll and authorize.** Accept only invited members, verify the Supabase user token, rate-limit submissions, and issue a submission identifier/challenge for online attempts. Keep offline attempts possible but label their capture time less strongly established. A nonce limits simple request replay; without attestation it cannot prove capture origin.
2. **Receive a bounded evidence bundle.** Include a format version; app/algorithm/config version; device model/OS; chosen vehicle; receiver identity when known; timestamps and monotonic-time anchors; all actual delivered location samples with speed/accuracy/position/course/altitude/source flags; motion, mount calibration and barometer samples needed for the claimed result; interruption/dropout markers. Do not upload only already-filtered peaks. A GPX replay/import is always tagged noncompetitive.
3. **Validate structure first.** Limit compressed and decompressed bytes, samples, duration, numeric ranges and nesting. Reject NaN/infinity, decreasing clocks, duplicate identifiers, impossible sample intervals, altered upload chunks and unsupported formats. Store a server-computed content hash to detect exact duplicates; that hash is not physical attestation.
4. **Recompute with the same versioned pure TypeScript measurement core used by the app.** The server chooses the accepted version/config; it does not accept a client’s arbitrary thresholds. Recalculate validity masks, sustained max speed, acceleration/braking crossings, complete lap gates, result uncertainty, and quality grade from the raw evidence. Never integrate across a tunnel gap and pretend the missing observations exist. Pin algorithm versions to records so later fixes can be audited/reprocessed.
5. **Apply eligibility and plausibility checks.** Reject explicit simulation/replay, insufficient reliable samples and incomplete courses. Check position/speed consistency, speed changes against motion with calibrated orientation, clock discontinuities, and improbable routes. Keep vehicle/engine-pattern suspicion separate from deterministic rejection. A threshold should flag review rather than certify a vehicle type.
6. **Decide once, transactionally.** Only trusted server code writes `accepted` status, metric values, country/course IDs and leaderboard entries. A user may create a pending submission but cannot edit its adjudication or overwrite another member’s evidence. Make resubmission idempotent. Record reason codes and support a report/review/reclassification path.

Supabase supports server logic and row-level policies, but secret/service-role credentials bypass RLS and must stay off the phone. Public clients use the publishable key with narrowly scoped policies. [Supabase security model](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/database/secure-data).

### Vehicle, country and ranking fairness

**Proposed policy:** vehicle class is declared and reviewed, never “automatically confirmed.” Keep bicycles and e-bikes separate; keep motorcycle and car separate; distinguish iPhone-only from external-receiver measurements. Store external receiver model/rate and measured update intervals. Separate timing method, rollout setting, course/layout/direction and algorithm version. Do not sort millisecond differences as meaningful when phone timing uncertainty overlaps; show ties/overlap or coarse precision. These are product rules to be finalized against measured uncertainty.

Define a country board as **country where the accepted performance took place**, preferably the administrator-reviewed country of its closed course. Profile country is a separate optional self-declaration. Do not derive the record country from SIM, locale, IP address or nationality. Reject or manually review ambiguous border-spanning events. This is our proposed definition, not a provider feature.

Strava’s published guidelines separate sport types and exclude motor-assisted activity from conventional cycling rankings; its experience is useful precedent for visible classification rules and corrections, not proof that our classifier will work. [Strava leaderboard guidelines](https://support.strava.com/en-us/articles/15401921-segment-leaderboard-guidelines).

### Login choice and recovery

**Initial recommendation: invited anonymous Supabase account + nickname.** Auth supplies a real user ID/JWT; an anonymous account is not the same as the public `anon` key. Authorize leaderboard participation only after the UID is associated with a valid invite/approved member. Persist the session in secure device storage; never use a nickname as proof of identity. Supabase documents that anonymous users lose access after sign-out, data clearing, or moving to another device; it supports later linking to a permanent method and applies an anonymous-signup IP rate limit (currently 30/hour). [Supabase anonymous accounts](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/auth/auth-anonymous).

**Recovery must be explicit:** for 1–10 known friends, use a documented administrator-assisted recovery process that verifies the friend outside the app and moves their public profile/results to a newly authenticated UID, then revokes the old membership. That migration/revocation would be custom server functionality, not a built-in anonymous-recovery API. Optionally implement a one-use high-entropy recovery code later, store only its hash, rotate it after use, and rate-limit attempts. Do not export refresh tokens inside ordinary ride reports. Keep local ride backup/restore separate from account recovery; restoring a GPX is not proof of ownership of a leaderboard profile.

**Email OTP is possible but not automatically free and ready.** Supabase’s default sender only sends to organization-team addresses and currently allows **2 messages/hour**. Do not add friends as backend project administrators to work around delivery restrictions. A custom SMTP provider is needed for normal users; any free SMTP quota, sender-domain requirements, renewal costs and delivery behavior must be verified separately before choosing it. Therefore email OTP is optional, not a baseline dependency. [Supabase SMTP restrictions](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/auth/auth-smtp).

These authentication methods use app/server networking and do not require Sign in with Apple, push delivery, or an Apple-associated-domain callback when using a manually entered code. Nickname-only local use remains available without a server.

## 22. Staying within free storage

### Storage arithmetic, not measured compression results

Record all delivered samples locally as requested. Use chunked binary/file storage plus SQLite metadata rather than huge JSON rows; preserve schema/version, actual sample rates and timestamp precision. The following **illustrative packed schema sizes** are design estimates, not library guarantees or measured output:

| Stream | Assumed representation | Calculation | Uncompressed payload/hour |
|---|---|---|---|
| Location | 96 bytes/sample, 1 Hz | 96 × 3,600 | 0.346 MB |
| Motion | 72 bytes/sample, 50 Hz; enough room for timestamp, quaternion, acceleration/gravity/rotation and flags | 72 × 50 × 3,600 | 12.960 MB |
| Barometer | 24 bytes/sample, 1 Hz | 24 × 3,600 | 0.086 MB |
| Total | Actual field choices may differ | Sum | **13.392 MB/hour**, before database/file/index overhead |

At 100 Hz the motion component doubles. If 10 riders record two hours/day, this model produces **267.84 MB/day** in aggregate; uploading everything would fill a 1 GB evidence allowance in roughly four days, before other data. The free allowance is documented by [Supabase pricing](https://supabase.com/pricing); the calculations above are our own. Compression may help, but no fixed compression ratio should be promised: noisy floating-point IMU streams are not the same as a compressible GPS polyline.

### Proposed upload/retention budget

- **Phone is the ride archive.** Upload only a candidate record’s complete measurement interval plus pre/post context (initially 30 seconds either side), its required raw samples, quality flags and course evidence. A lap requires the entire lap. The server receives enough evidence to recompute the claimed interval; a clip cannot validate the rest of a ride.
- **Initial submission limit:** 2 MB compressed and 8 MB decompressed per verification unit, plus a maximum duration/sample count; profile before choosing final limits. Longer laps require sequential chunks with server-maintained filter state and a final completeness check, or are explicitly unsupported for ranking until that path is tested. Never silently downsample raw evidence to satisfy a cap.
- **Initial admission target:** at most 5 candidate uploads/member/day and **250 KB average** after measured compression; these are proposed limits/targets, not achieved sizes. Ten members × 5 × 30 × 0.25 MB = **375 MB of new evidence/month**. With the owner plus 10 friends, use 11 members: **412.5 MB/month** at that average. At the 2 MB hard ceiling, ten members could produce **3 GB/month**, so rate limiting alone does not bound storage: enforce total bytes too.
- **Retention model:** unaccepted/pending/rejected blobs for 7 days; accepted ordinary evidence 30 days; pin evidence for current best records and records under dispute. Retain only small summaries/hash/version/decision metadata after expiry. Pinning needs a byte cap (e.g. 250 MB) and admission policy; an unbounded set of courses or all-time winners will otherwise grow forever.
- **Proposed soft stop:** stop new admissions at 700 MB object usage or 350 MB database usage and explain that recording still works locally. Reserve room for in-flight uploads, indexes, Auth metadata, cleanup and review. Free allocation is not a target to fill exactly.
- **Evidence expiry:** a stored result with deleted evidence is no longer independently recomputable from the server. Mark it archived/evidence expired, exclude it from actively challengeable rankings, or request consented re-upload from the original device. A hash cannot reconstruct deleted measurements.
- **No public raw files.** Store evidence in private buckets with owner/reviewer-only access. Public leaderboard responses contain only nickname, run-country/course, metric, coarse date and quality/method labels. Do not expose signed evidence URLs in public APIs.

Retention, caps and admission rules above are proposed product policy. Supabase’s storage authorization is built on RLS; define both table and object policies and verify unauthorized reads fail. [Storage access control](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/storage/security/access-control).

For capacity testing, measure packed bytes/hour and compressed bytes for at least a static session, a rough bicycle ride, a motorcycle/car ride, and a long lap. Measure decompression+validation CPU and peak memory at the hard upload ceiling, including malformed input. Target comfortable headroom below the provider limit, not a p50 result just below 2 seconds. Track storage, egress, function invocation count and database size in a monthly export. Ten users repeatedly downloading raw data can exhaust egress sooner than summary queries; do not download evidence just to display a board.

Keep backups under the user’s control on Windows and test restore. A free paused backend and an anonymous session are not backups. Local files should survive ordinary app updates where identity/container continuity is preserved, but reinstall/re-signing behavior belongs to the separate installation test gate; do not infer data safety from the backend choice.

## 23. Safety and privacy

### Closed-course performance rankings

**Proposed default:** ordinary riding records private speed/statistics, but public top-speed, 0–100, 100–0 and competitive lap boards require an **administrator-approved closed course and an approved session**. A racetrack polygon alone does not prove the venue was closed or authorized on that day; a user tapping “closed course” is insufficient. Review course layout/direction, safe run-off and operating context with the people running the test. Unknown/public-road runs are ineligible for public performance records rather than a second public “street speed” board.

Disable live rank chasing, celebratory new-record haptics and target prompts on public-road mode. Performance setup and results review occur while stopped. Do not reward maximum braking G or extreme cornering on ordinary roads. Add a simple hazard/report path; a flagged course can suspend new awards pending review. Strava similarly removes achievement incentives from hazardous segments, providing useful precedent, though its waiver-based restoration policy need not be copied. [Strava hazardous-segment behavior](https://support.strava.com/en-us/articles/15402025-how-do-i-flag-a-segment-as-hazardous-on-strava).

Course eligibility improves incentives and context; it does not make high-speed tests safe by itself. This report does not establish legal speed/access limits or certify a venue. Final access rules and local conditions must come from the venue/operator and applicable official sources at the time of testing.

### Privacy zones are useful but incomplete

Keep all rides **private by default**. Publishing a record should not publish the full route. For this small friend test, default public profiles to nickname plus optional broad country, and publish approved course identity rather than a rider’s exact start/end address. Strava’s own documentation distinguishes map hiding from activity visibility and warns that hidden locations can still be inferred. A security research paper demonstrates endpoint-privacy-zone inference attacks. [Strava map visibility](https://support.strava.com/en-us/articles/15402012-how-do-i-edit-my-map-visibility-on-strava), [USENIX endpoint privacy analysis](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity18/presentation/hassan).

**Proposed implementation rules:**

1. Let users create home/work/sensitive-location zones locally, plus a start/end distance trim. A suggested initial 500 m trim is a UI default to test, not a privacy guarantee. Support hiding the whole map with one action.
2. Clip **every** segment that crosses a zone, including mid-ride visits. Split polylines at hidden spans; never draw a straight connector through a hidden location. Do not publish zone centers/radii, hidden coordinates in bounds, or hidden timestamps/chart samples as metadata.
3. Generate sanitized public geometry on the trusted publishing path. Merely hiding pixels in the app leaves coordinates downloadable. Apply the same policy to static images, GPX sharing, map thumbnails and chart-linked points; show a preview before publishing/exporting.
4. Keep full local raw data separate from sanitized shares. If a performance interval overlaps a sensitive zone, keep that result private unless the user deliberately submits the required evidence; explain exactly what the backend reviewer can see. Privacy masking before verification can remove necessary evidence and must not be disguised as a fully checked run.
5. Do not display live locations or exact departure/return times. Repeated routes and precise distance/time patterns may still identify a home even after clipping; hiding the entire route is the stronger option. A closed-course result need only expose its venue and coarse date.
6. Provide delete/export controls for local rides, uploaded evidence and public records, with clear distinctions. Device logs and test reports also contain precise location; their export screen must identify that content and give a redacted option. Do not collect a friend’s contact list or publish a stable hardware identifier.

These are recommended safeguards, not a claim that a particular radius anonymizes a route. Strava’s default endpoint hiding and exclusion of hidden segment efforts from public boards illustrate the privacy/ranking interaction. [Strava map visibility and hidden efforts](https://support.strava.com/en-us/articles/15402012-how-do-i-edit-my-map-visibility-on-strava).

## Community evidence and remaining gates

Community reports are separated from provider guarantees:

- A July–September 2025 Stack Overflow thread reports `.cycling` missing in Xcode 16.4 but present in Xcode 26 with an iOS 14 availability annotation. It explains the SDK/documentation trap but is not an iOS 17 runtime test. [Report](https://stackoverflow.com/questions/79714435/mapkit-mkdirections-cycling-is-available).
- A Thailand-based ORS motorcycle user described car/bicycle profiles choosing unsuitable alternatives; a provider maintainer confirmed that a dedicated motorcycle profile was not planned then. This is qualitative local experience and a dated maintainer response, checked against today’s documented profile list. [Discussion](https://ask.openrouteservice.org/t/adding-motorcycle-profile/3928).
- User complaints that D1 exceeded Free limits before enforcement should not be treated as extra entitlement; the provider now explicitly documents enforcement from September 1, 2026. [Earlier anecdote](https://www.reddit.com/r/CloudFlare/comments/1v9zpvf/d1_keeps_writing_past_the_free_plans_100k_rowsday/), [current changelog](https://developers.cloudflare.com/changelog/product/d1/).

Before Phase 5/6 implementation, the unresolved gates are: actual iOS 17 native cycling behavior with the chosen modern Xcode; Thailand route correctness and motorcycle access; permitted hosted routing use under current terms; measured evidence size/compression and verifier CPU; tested account recovery and signed-app update continuity; approved course/session policy; and a privacy-export audit. None requires starting product implementation in Phase 1. No paid plan or provider account was created in this research.
